About Me

I completed my undergraduate and master degrees in molecular and cellular biology (with biotechnology) at the University of Glasgow. During my first year in undergraduate I won a scholarship to undertake a summer placement which I chose to do in the molecular genetics lab. This inspired me to look for a placement in a lab where I could further these skills.

Through my leftures I became particarly interested in molecular plant microbe interactions. This led me to chosethe Tree and Microbe Interaction (IAM) lab, in Nancy France for the 12 month placement required for my master programme. The project was supervised by Dr Benjamin Petre and involved characteristion of a family of plant defence peptides, called the rust induced secreted peptides, using ancestral sequence reconstruction.

I am currently researching for my PhD at The Sainsbury Laboratory, Norwich. I am working in the Proteomics Team under Dr Frank Menke on MAPK regulation in plant pathogenic fungi using Magnaporthe oryzae as a model system. The primary goal of the project is to use functional genomics to drive discovery of novel regatory mechanisms of MAPK involved in pathogenicity.

In my spare time, I like to photograph fungi, which I document on iNaturalist.
